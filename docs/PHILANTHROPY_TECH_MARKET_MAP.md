# Philanthropy Technology Market Map

**Working definition:** Philanthropy technology is software and infrastructure that helps people and institutions source, move, manage, deploy, measure, or govern capital intended for public benefit.

This definition is intentionally narrower than all nonprofit technology and broader than grantmaking software. It covers the full capital lifecycle while treating mission-specific service-delivery software as an adjacent market.

## How To Read The Market

The market is easiest to understand across two axes:

1. **Capital lifecycle:** create wealth -> commit funds -> move funds -> select recipients -> manage awards -> measure outcomes -> report and govern.
2. **Primary customer:** individual donor, institutional funder, intermediary/advisor, nonprofit/grantee, or ecosystem/regulator.

AI, data infrastructure, payments, and implementation services are cross-cutting capabilities. They should not be treated as standalone verticals unless the product has a distinct buyer, workflow, and budget.

## Market Overview

| Layer | Vertical | Core jobs to be done | Typical buyers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core philanthropy | 1. Philanthropic planning and structuring | Decide how, when, and through which vehicle to give | Individuals, families, advisors |
| Core philanthropy | 2. Giving vehicles and administration | Establish and operate DAFs, foundations, trusts, and pooled funds | Donors, foundations, sponsors |
| Core philanthropy | 3. Giving and disbursement rails | Accept, process, match, route, and disburse charitable funds | Nonprofits, employers, platforms, funders |
| Core philanthropy | 4. Nonprofit discovery and due diligence | Find, compare, validate, and monitor potential recipients | Donors, funders, advisors |
| Core philanthropy | 5. Institutional grantmaking | Source, review, approve, administer, and close grants | Foundations, corporate giving teams, governments |
| Core philanthropy | 6. Philanthropic portfolio operations | Manage grants, investments, compliance, tax, governance, and reporting together | Foundations, family offices |
| Core philanthropy | 7. Impact measurement and learning | Define, collect, analyze, and communicate outcomes | Funders, nonprofits, impact investors |
| Core philanthropy | 8. Impact capital and alternative finance | Deploy capital through investments and nontraditional instruments | Foundations, family offices, impact investors |
| Nonprofit side | 9. Fundraising and donor engagement | Acquire, retain, cultivate, and steward supporters | Nonprofits, schools, faith organizations |
| Nonprofit side | 10. Grantseeking and award management | Find funding, submit proposals, and manage awarded funds | Nonprofits, universities, researchers |
| Nonprofit side | 11. Nonprofit finance and operations | Run the organization's finances, people, compliance, and internal work | Nonprofits |
| Nonprofit side | 12. Program and constituent operations | Deliver services and manage volunteers, members, and beneficiaries | Nonprofits, public agencies |

## Vertical Detail

### 1. Philanthropic Planning And Structuring

- Charitable tax planning and deduction optimization
- Estate, legacy, and planned-giving design
- Giving strategy, values discovery, and family decision-making
- Charitable asset liquidation, including stock, crypto, real estate, and private shares
- Advisor tools for wealth managers, attorneys, and accountants
- Vehicle selection and formation workflows

This vertical ends when a giving vehicle is operational. Ongoing vehicle administration belongs in vertical 2.

### 2. Giving Vehicles And Administration

- Donor-advised fund administration
- Private and family foundation administration
- Community foundation administration
- Charitable trusts and planned-gift administration
- Fiscal sponsorship
- Pooled funds, giving circles, and philanthropic collaboratives
- White-label charitable account infrastructure and philanthropy-as-a-service

Key workflows include onboarding, contributions, fund accounting, grant recommendations, disbursement, statements, governance, and tax reporting.

### 3. Giving And Disbursement Rails

- Online donation forms and payment processing
- Recurring giving and subscription-style donations
- Peer-to-peer fundraising and crowdfunding
- Workplace giving, payroll giving, and employee matching
- Corporate rewards, checkout giving, and embedded giving
- Noncash and complex-asset donations
- Cross-border giving, equivalency determination, and regranting
- Grant and aid disbursement, including cards, ACH, and direct cash transfers
- Receipting, acknowledgments, and reconciliation

The defining product is a transaction rail. Donor engagement around those transactions belongs in vertical 9.

### 4. Nonprofit Discovery And Due Diligence

- Nonprofit search, profiles, and recommendation engines
- Charity ratings and effectiveness research
- Funder and grantee relationship intelligence
- Eligibility, tax-status, and good-standing validation
- Financial health and governance analysis
- Sanctions, fraud, adverse-media, and reputational screening
- Site visits, diligence workflows, and document collection
- Ongoing recipient monitoring and alerts

### 5. Institutional Grantmaking

- Grants management systems
- Applicant portals and eligibility screening
- Application intake and workflow automation
- Reviewer, committee, and board decision workflows
- Grant agreements and e-signatures
- Milestones, reporting, amendments, and renewals
- Grant payments and payment scheduling
- Grantee relationship management
- Participatory, trust-based, and collaborative grantmaking
- Government and research grant administration

### 6. Philanthropic Portfolio Operations

- Unified grant, organization, and relationship records
- Foundation and family-office workflow management
- Endowment and investment portfolio tracking
- Mission-related and program-related investment tracking
- Private-foundation payout calculations and compliance
- Tax records and regulatory filings
- Board governance, approvals, and meeting materials
- Budgeting, scenario planning, and cash-flow forecasting
- Portfolio analytics, dashboards, and stakeholder reporting
- Document management, task orchestration, and audit trails

This is the clearest current home for Benevolence. Its opportunity is to become the operating system that connects decisions, grants, investments, tax, compliance, and impact rather than competing as a narrow grants management system.

### 7. Impact Measurement And Learning

- Theory-of-change and logic-model design
- Metric, indicator, and KPI management
- Grantee and program data collection
- Monitoring, evaluation, and learning workflows
- Outcome attribution and contribution analysis
- Social return on investment and impact valuation
- Beneficiary feedback and participatory evaluation
- Impact dashboards and narrative reporting
- Benchmarking and evidence repositories
- ESG, sustainability, and regulatory impact reporting where it overlaps with philanthropy

### 8. Impact Capital And Alternative Finance

- Impact-investing portfolio management
- Mission-related investments and program-related investments
- Recoverable grants and forgivable loans
- Guarantees, catalytic capital, and blended finance
- Outcomes-based financing and social-impact bonds
- Community investment notes and lending
- Nonprofit loan funds and underwriting
- Secondary markets and liquidity for impact assets

### 9. Fundraising And Donor Engagement

- Constituent and donor CRM
- Donation pages and campaign management
- Major-gift and moves management
- Prospect research and wealth screening
- Email, SMS, direct mail, and supporter journeys
- Events, auctions, galas, and ticketing
- Membership, alumni, and annual-giving programs
- Peer-to-peer and ambassador fundraising
- Recurring-gift retention and upgrade optimization
- Stewardship, acknowledgments, and donor reporting
- Fundraising intelligence, forecasting, and AI assistance

### 10. Grantseeking And Award Management

- Grant and request-for-proposal discovery
- Funder research and relationship management
- Proposal writing and content libraries
- Application calendars, pipelines, and collaboration
- Budget preparation and internal approvals
- Submission portals and standardized applications
- Post-award project, restriction, and deliverable tracking
- Funder reporting and renewal management
- Government grant compliance and indirect-cost management
- Research administration

Grantmaker-side administration belongs in vertical 5; applicant-side revenue operations belong here.

### 11. Nonprofit Finance And Operations

- Fund accounting and restricted-fund management
- Budgeting, forecasting, treasury, and cash flow
- Accounts payable, expense, procurement, and spend management
- Payroll, HR, benefits, and workforce management
- Board governance and entity management
- Tax-exempt filings, charitable registration, and compliance
- Contract, document, and policy management
- Cybersecurity, IT administration, privacy, and risk
- General CRM, project management, and collaboration adapted for nonprofits

### 12. Program And Constituent Operations

- Case and client management
- Benefits navigation and referral management
- Volunteer recruitment, scheduling, and engagement
- Membership and chapter management
- Advocacy, organizing, and civic engagement
- Education, mentoring, and learning delivery
- Humanitarian response and aid logistics
- Community engagement and participatory decision-making
- Service delivery, attendance, and outcome tracking

This vertical quickly branches into mission-specific markets such as health, housing, education, food access, climate, and humanitarian aid. Those mission areas are segments, not philanthropy-tech verticals in their own right.

### 13. Data, Trust, And Transparency Infrastructure

- Nonprofit identity and entity-resolution services
- Tax filings, grant records, and open-data platforms
- Sector benchmarks and market intelligence
- Eligibility, validation, and compliance APIs
- Fraud, risk, and anomaly detection
- Consent, privacy, and responsible-data infrastructure
- Interoperability standards and shared taxonomies
- Data warehouses, integration platforms, and migration tools
- Public transparency and accountability portals

### 14. Networks, Marketplaces, And Collaborative Giving

- Nonprofit and project marketplaces
- Giving circles and pooled-fund platforms
- Funder collaboratives and co-funding networks
- Participatory budgeting and community allocation
- Skills-based volunteering and pro-bono marketplaces
- Corporate-nonprofit partnership marketplaces
- Donation matching and challenge platforms
- Shared-services networks and nonprofit purchasing

Network effects, rather than workflow depth, are the defining advantage in this vertical.

### 15. Enablement And Implementation

- Software selection and advisory
- Implementation and managed administration
- Data migration, cleansing, and enrichment
- Systems integration and API orchestration
- Training, change management, and digital capacity building
- Managed security, privacy, and compliance
- Outsourced grantmaking, foundation, and nonprofit operations
- Technology discounts and procurement

Although often service-led, this vertical increasingly includes repeatable software and AI-enabled managed services.

## Cross-Cutting Capabilities

These capabilities appear in nearly every vertical and should be mapped as overlays:

- **AI and automation:** research, drafting, recommendations, workflow execution, anomaly detection, and conversational interfaces
- **Payments and financial infrastructure:** card, ACH, ledgering, custody, reconciliation, and cross-border movement
- **Data and integrations:** APIs, warehouses, entity resolution, imports, and interoperability
- **Compliance and risk:** permissions, audit trails, sanctions, privacy, filings, and controls
- **Collaboration and workflow:** tasks, approvals, documents, notifications, and portals
- **Analytics and reporting:** dashboards, benchmarking, forecasting, and generated narratives

## Buyer Segments

Buyer segment is a separate dimension from product vertical:

- Individual donors and households
- High-net-worth and ultra-high-net-worth families
- Family offices and philanthropic advisors
- Private, family, community, and corporate foundations
- Corporate social-impact and employee-engagement teams
- Government agencies and multilateral funders
- Donor-advised fund sponsors and other intermediaries
- Operating nonprofits, segmented by size and mission
- Universities, hospitals, faith organizations, and associations
- Consultants, accountants, attorneys, and wealth managers

## Market-Mapping Dataset

For a useful vendor-level map, record these fields for every company:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Company and product | Identify the offering, not only the parent company |
| Primary vertical and subvertical | Place it in the taxonomy |
| Secondary verticals | Expose suite expansion and convergence |
| Primary buyer and organization size | Distinguish apparently similar competitors |
| User persona | Identify who actually adopts and operates it |
| Geography | Capture regulatory and cross-border constraints |
| Business model | SaaS, transaction fee, assets under management, services, data, or hybrid |
| System of record | Determine whether the product owns durable data |
| Money movement | Mark whether it directly controls transactions |
| Network effects | Mark marketplaces and multi-sided platforms |
| AI capability | Distinguish assistance, insight, and autonomous execution |
| Integrations | Identify ecosystem position and switching costs |
| Ownership and funding | Track independents, consolidators, nonprofits, and incumbents |
| Evidence | Product URL, pricing, customer examples, and last verification date |

## Benevolence Positioning

### Current Wedge

Benevolence sits primarily in **philanthropic portfolio operations**, with meaningful coverage in:

- Institutional grantmaking
- Impact measurement and learning
- Nonprofit discovery and due diligence
- Philanthropic planning, tax, and compliance
- Donor and pledge management
- Data integration and AI-enabled workflow automation

### Strategic White Space

The strongest strategic position is not “another grants management system.” It is:

> **An AI-native operating system for foundations and philanthropic family offices that manages the whole portfolio of grants, investments, compliance obligations, relationships, and impact.**

That position joins workflows that are commonly fragmented across grants software, spreadsheets, accounting systems, investment reports, tax advisors, and board documents.

The most defensible expansion paths are:

1. Deepen the foundation and family-office system of record.
2. Connect grantmaking decisions to tax, payout, liquidity, and investment context.
3. Build reliable impact and portfolio intelligence from operational data.
4. Become the action layer across specialist systems through integrations and AI workflows.
5. Add multi-party network features only after owning the funder-side workflow.

## Boundaries And Common Mapping Errors

- Do not treat every nonprofit SaaS product as philanthropy tech.
- Do not mix buyer segments, such as “corporate philanthropy,” with functional verticals.
- Do not mix mission areas, such as climate or education, with software categories.
- Do not classify AI as a vertical when it is merely a feature.
- Separate grantmaker-side grants management from grantseeker-side grant management.
- Separate transaction rails from donor CRM and engagement.
- Separate impact measurement from general reporting.
- Record suites in every vertical they actually serve, but assign one primary wedge.
